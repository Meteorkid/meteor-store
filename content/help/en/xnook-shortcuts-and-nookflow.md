## Keyboard shortcuts

These work whenever the panel is open — you do not need to click a region first to give it focus:

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `⌘0` | Back to the overview |
| `⌘1` – `⌘4` | Switch to the 1st–4th **enabled** tool, in the order shown in Settings |
| `⌘N` | Create a new note |
| `Esc` | Step back one level; collapse when already on the overview |

`⌘1`–`⌘4` follow the enabled list, not a fixed slot. If you turn off the file tray, the third shortcut moves to the next tool that is still on.

## Opening the panel

By default, a two-finger scroll down on the trackpad expands it. If that fires too easily, switch to hover expansion in Settings and the pill opens when the cursor rests on it.

Neither mode changes the shortcuts: once the panel is open, the keyboard behaves identically.

## NookFlow: tasks, notes and files in one thread

NookFlow exists for the case where a piece of work is half done and its notes and files are scattered across three places. There are two entry points:

- **Start manually** — create a task directly inside NookFlow;
- **Start from an event** — a task seeded by a calendar event, carrying its time across with it.

Once a task exists you can attach notes and files to it. Attachments are references, not copies: the file stays where it is, and editing a note edits that same note. After the task ends you can reopen it from history and pull the notes and files back. That is the difference from a sticky-note app: it records context, not just text.

## Exporting and deleting notes

Notes are searchable by title and body, and can be renamed. Deletion has an undo window, so a mis-click does not send you to a backup.

For moving content out there are two export formats: Markdown and plain text. Markdown keeps heading levels and list structure, which is what you want when importing into another notes tool; plain text drops all markup, which is what you want when pasting into an email or a chat window.

## Running both X Nook and X Island

No conflict. Both apps occupy the same pill at the top of the screen, but only one is shown at a time. A two-finger horizontal swipe switches between them, and switching is an explicit action — an incoming notification will not steal focus for you.

If you would rather keep just one, X Island alone is enough for managing AI agents. X Nook is a companion tool centre, not a component of X Island; skipping it does not affect how X Island works.

## Panel will not expand, or shortcuts do nothing

1. **Check whether something is covering it.** Both pills sit at the top centre and are hidden by the system menu bar in full-screen mode. Leave full screen and try again.
2. **Check the expansion mode.** After switching to scroll-only, hovering no longer opens the panel — that is expected.
3. **Shortcuts need the panel to have focus.** While the panel is collapsed, `⌘1`–`⌘4` do not reach across applications. That is deliberate: the app does not hijack global shortcuts.
4. **Multi-display behaviour.** The pill follows the display your cursor is on. If you cannot find it, move the cursor first.

## Motion and power

The bounce on entry, the magnetic pull near the cursor and the scrolling text can each be tuned or turned off in Settings. When macOS has Reduce Motion enabled, the app follows the system setting and quiets decorative animation without changing what the tools do.
